# Mimic-Phon

An original AU / VST3 effect for Logic Pro, inspired by the feel of a Eurorack stereo multi-zone colour repeater (the
Make Noise / soundhack Mimeophon): eight Zones of delay time from a millisecond to over forty seconds, a Rate that
moves smoothly inside each Zone, repeats up to infinite, and colour and stereo smear on the repeats. All DSP is our own
code; it is not a port or copy of anything, and it is not affiliated with Make Noise or soundhack.

**Status: version 0.9.0 (milestone 9: Halo). 0.9.0 has been checked on a Linux test computer and by GitHub's Mac build; it is not yet tested in Logic.**
Version 0.8.0 (Tempo Sync of Rate) was tested in Logic on both test Macs on 7 November 2026 and worked as described, so **milestone 8 is finished**. Milestone 7 (0.7.0, uRate and its LFO) and milestones 1 to 6 were finished on 6 November 2026 (0.6.0 is Skew, Ping Pong and Swap, 0.5.0 Color, 0.4.1 the core repeater, 0.3.1 the styled panel, 0.2.0 the empty build).
**The plugin makes sound: Input Level and its gain step, Zone, Rate, Repeats, Mix, Output Level, Clear, Color, Skew, Ping Pong / Swap, uRate with its LFO, Tempo Sync of Rate and now Halo work.** Flip and Hold are still silent: they are built in the next milestones.

## First five minutes

1. Install it (see *Getting the plugin* below) and put **MimicPhon** on an audio track: Audio FX > Audio Units >
   particledots > MimicPhon. It works on mono and stereo tracks. Use something with clear gaps in it (drums, a plucked or played
   phrase) and press Play.
2. **Starting positions:** you hear the track plus an echo about two thirds of a second later, which dies away over about five
   seconds. The window says **653 ms** (Zone 4, Rate in the middle) and Repeats **x 0.35** (each echo is a third as loud as the one before).
3. **Mix:** all the way left gives only the original sound, all the way right gives only the echoes.
4. **Repeats:** all the way left gives a single echo; turning right gives more and more. Near the top there is a short stretch that reads
   **infinite** (the echoes never die away); past it, **inf +12 %** at the very end, the echoes grow and fill up until they saturate, but they never run away. Turn Repeats back down to calm it.
5. **Rate:** turn it slowly. The delay time moves smoothly inside the Zone (the window shows it) and, with Repeats up, the echoes bend in pitch
   while it moves (doppler); it settles back to the normal pitch a moment after you stop.
6. **Zone:** step through the eight Zones. The echo time jumps at once, **without** a pitch bend (a short crossfade of about a fiftieth of a second).
   Zone 0 and 1 (1.3 to 82 ms) give buzzing, flanging and ringing tones rather than echoes; Zones 2 to 4 are ordinary echoes; Zones 5 to 7 are long (up to 41.8 seconds).
   Try Zone 7 with Rate to the right: whatever you play comes back almost 42 seconds later.
7. **Input Level**, the **-3 / 0 / +3 / +6 dB** chips under it, and **Output Level**: how loud the signal going in and coming out is.
8. **Clear:** press CLEAR while echoes are ringing: they stop at once, in every Zone, and new sound goes in and comes back as before. (The button lights for a third of a second.)
9. Stop and start Logic's playback, and bounce a short piece: Logic is told how long the echoes keep sounding with the current settings.
10. Save the Logic project, close it and open it again: the controls come back where you left them.
11. **Color** (new in 0.5.0): the starting position is **unity** (the knob's 3:00): the repeats are not coloured at all. Turn it left and the repeats go darker and darker (the window says *tape*, *crunch BBD*, *warm BBD*, *oil can*): the highs close up and the sound gets a little saturated, and **each echo is darker than the one before** (try Repeats near the top). Turn it right of unity and the repeats get thinner and crisper (*crisp*, *thin*): the lows fall away echo by echo. Try Mix fully right with Repeats fully left: you hear one echo, coloured, which is a simple tone filter. Turning Color slowly should not click.
12. **Skew** (new in 0.6.0): press SKEW with Rate at 12:00 (the Rate card has a teal mark there): nothing changes, the two sides are still the same delay. Now turn **Rate clockwise: the Left echo comes sooner and the Right echo later** (the window's Left / Right read-out shows the two times and the Rate card says *L faster 40 %*); counter-clockwise does the opposite. With Repeats up you hear the two sides drift apart into a wide, phased echo; in a mono track it widens the sound (use a stereo track). Press SKEW off: **nothing jumps**, the split stays, and Rate now moves both sides together. Press SKEW on and off again **without touching Rate** and the split is cleared. Try slow Rate turns in Zones 0 and 1 for flanging and chorus-like effects.
13. **Ping Pong / Swap** (new in 0.6.0): with Skew off, press PING PONG / SWAP (the button then says **PING PONG** and the Zone window flashes): the left and right inputs are mixed into one, and the echoes **bounce Left, Right, Left ...**, the first one on the left. Each bounce is half the delay time (the read-out shows each hop; the Rate card still shows the whole time). With the sides skewed, the same button says **SWAP**: the left and right feedback paths are swapped, so a stereo input gives a phase-shifted pair of echo patterns. Use Repeats around 12:00 to hear more than one bounce.
14. **uRate and its LFO** (new in 0.7.0): the **uRate** knob is a small doppler control. Leave it in the middle (nothing happens), then turn it: in Zones 1 to 7 it makes the delay up to 6 ms shorter (clockwise) or longer, and while you turn it the echoes bend in pitch; in **Zone 0** it is a pitch control (+-12 semitones, one volt per octave): put Repeats up and play a click or a burst of noise in for plucked-string tones. The **uRate LFO** swings it for you: turn **Depth** up (the first half of the knob is a gentle wobble, the far end is very deep) and **Rate** from slow to fast, **Sine** or **Triangle**. A gentle chorus is Depth around 12:00 with Rate around 10:00 in Zone 1 or 2 and Repeats around 12:00. With **Skew** on and the sides apart, the right side swings the opposite way (stereo chorus) in Zones 1 to 7. **Sync** makes the LFO follow Logic's tempo (Rate then picks a note division and the card says which, for example *1/4*) and lock to the song position. The cards say what they do: the uRate card reads *6.0 ms shorter*, the Depth reads the swing in milliseconds (or semitones in Zone 0), and the Left / Right delay box includes uRate's steady offset.
15. **Tempo Sync** (new in 0.8.0): press TEMPO SYNC (it lights teal). Rate now picks one of **13 steps** of Logic's tempo instead of a free time, and the Rate card names the step and the clock: **x2** at the far left, **x1 3/4, x1 2/3, x1 1/2, x1 1/3, x1 1/4**, then **1/1** around 12:30 (the teal mark, the repeat is exactly one clock period), then **/1 1/4, /1 1/3, /1 1/2, /1 2/3, /1 3/4** and **/2** at the far right. At the starting positions (Zone 4, Rate in the middle, Clock Division 1/4, 120 BPM) the window reads **1/1 (tempo 1/4)** and **500 ms**. **Turning Rate jumps from step to step with no pitch bend** (a short crossfade of about a fiftieth of a second, the same as a Zone change), so a sweep gives rhythmic patterns rather than a swoop. **Zone** doubles or halves the time per step of the Zone knob (Zone 5 = 1.00 s, Zone 3 = 250 ms with the same Rate), and **Clock Division** (2 bars ... 1/32, starting at 1/4) says what note value the clock ticks at, so 1/4T gives triplet times. With **Skew** on, Rate pulls the two sides onto different steps (the card reads for example *L x1 2/3  R /1 2/3*, the Delay box *L 300 ms, R 833 ms*): two multiples at once. **uRate and its LFO still bend the pitch** while Tempo Sync is on (that is what the module's microRate does too). Press TEMPO SYNC off: Rate goes back to the free time and glides again.
16. **Halo** (new in 0.9.0): the starting position is fully left, **off**: nothing changes. Turn it up a little (*glint*, *string*): the repeats get a short, ringing smear, almost pitched at first. In the middle (*tight verb*) it is a tight, Karplus-like little reverb; towards the right (*room*, *wide hall*) it grows into a big reverb with a wide stereo image, and at the very end it rings for a few seconds but always dies away. It also works with **Repeats fully left and Mix fully right** (one echo and its halo; with Zone 0 and Rate fully left it is almost a reverb straight on the input, the module's Filter-Verb patch). Turn **Color** while Halo is up: dark gives a dark reverb, thin a thin, bright one. Above about 55 % the halo is also fed back with the repeats (more Repeats = a denser wash). Turn Halo back to the left and the reverb fades out in about a third of a second.
17. **Flip and Hold are expected to do nothing yet.**

## The window

Laid out like the approved picture (milestone 1); it can be resized (the 3:2 shape stays).

- **Zone window and staircase:** the big square shows the selected Zone in its colour (0 green, 1 lime, 2 yellow, 3 orange, 4 red,
  5 magenta, 6 lilac, 7 blue); the staircase beside it lists the eight Zones with their ranges and lights the selected one. The
  square dims and brightens, half a second each way, while **Ping Pong / Swap** is working (it does not on a mono track).
- **Delay and tempo box:** the **Left and Right** delay times (from the engine's own Skew and Zone functions, so these are the times you hear: equal normally, different with Skew, half of the Rate time each with Ping Pong), Logic's tempo, and the TICK and RATE lights (the RATE light blinks for both sides when they differ).
  The box includes uRate's steady offset (the LFO swings around it and is not shown).
  With Tempo Sync on the box shows the clocked time of each side (the Rate card names the steps). If Logic has not told the plugin a tempo, the box says *TEMPO 120.0 (assumed)* and the clock is 120 BPM.
- **Ten knob cards and the Zone knob:** Input Level (with the -3 / 0 / +3 / +6 dB chips), Mix, Repeats, Halo, Output Level; Zone
  (eight steps, the arc takes the Zone's colour), Color (teal mark at 3:00, "unity", the position that does nothing), Rate (teal mark at 12:00, "1/1"), uRate,
  and the uRate LFO card (Rate, Depth, Sine / Triangle, Sync). Below: Hold, Flip, Skew, Ping Pong / Swap, Clear, Tempo Sync (teal when
  on) and the Clock Division menu.
- **Read-outs:** the ones for controls that make sound now come from the engine's own functions, so the number shown is the number heard: Input Level and
  Output Level in dB, Mix as a percentage wet, **Repeats** as "one echo", "x 0.35" (how much of each echo is fed back), "infinite" and "inf +12 %", **Color** as a word ("oil can", "warm BBD", "crunch BBD", "tape", "clean", "crisp", "thin"; the engine's own function, "clean" = unity), and the delay time. The others are still first guesses until their milestone
  **Halo** as a word and a percentage (the engine's own function: "off", "glint", "string", "tight verb", "room", "wide hall").
- **Rate card and Ping Pong / Swap button:** with the sides skewed the Rate card reads *L faster 40 %* (or *R faster*); otherwise it reads the delay time. The Ping Pong / Swap button says **PING PONG** or **SWAP** while it is working and **PING PONG / SWAP** while it is off.

## Things worth knowing

- **How the repeater works (so the controls make sense):** everything you play is written into one long stereo memory (41.8 seconds, about 16 MB at 48 kHz) that all eight Zones share; a Zone is only a different delay time to read it at. That is why switching to a longer Zone can bring back sound from many seconds ago.
- **Rate glides, Zone jumps.** Rate moves the delay time smoothly (it takes about a twelfth of a second to settle), so the echoes bend in pitch while you turn it. A Zone change jumps to the new time with a short crossfade (a fiftieth of a second) and no pitch effect.
- **Repeats beyond infinite:** the feedback goes slightly above 100 % at the far end, and a soft limiter in the feedback keeps it from running away: it fills up and saturates. At **infinite**, sound louder than about -2 dB is held down a little at each pass until it sits just under that level, while quieter sound goes round forever.
- **Color vs the real module (known difference, not changed):** the module's Color is described as a tilt-style filter that is fully open at fully clockwise and gets brighter, and slightly louder, going right. Here the right-hand side thins out with a rising high-pass instead. 0.5.0 was approved by ear, so it is left alone until you ask for it to be brought closer.
- **Color, in detail (first guesses, tuned by ear later):** left of unity, a saturation that grows towards the left (soft, small sounds are unchanged) followed by a 2-pole low-pass that closes from 17 kHz to 700 Hz at the far left; right of unity, a little saturation followed by a 2-pole high-pass that rises from 20 Hz to 1.5 kHz at the far right. Within 0.02 of unity (0.78 to 0.82) **nothing is done at all** and the sound is bit for bit the same as without Color; it fades in over the next 0.05. The filters are written into the repeater with the sound, so every echo is shaped once more than the one before and what you turn is baked into what comes back later (Zones 5 to 7 included). Mix fully left is never coloured: the live signal is untouched. The filters cannot boost anything, so Color cannot make the repeats run away; the soft limiter and the leveller are still there as before.
- **Skew, in detail (first guesses, tuned by ear later):** the two sides are centred where Rate was when Skew went on; the split is `Rate - 12:00` in knob units each way (so at the far ends the two sides are half the Zone's knob travel either side of the centre, a factor of 4 in delay time in Zones 1 to 6). Rate moving while Skew is on glides the two times like any Rate move (doppler); pressing Skew on away from 12:00, or clearing a split, makes the times jump and the engine crossfades (no pitch bend). "Skewed" means the sides differ by more than 1 % of the knob (back to "not skewed" below 0.5 %): that is what decides whether the button means Ping Pong or Swap.
- **Ping Pong, in detail (a first guess):** the two inputs are averaged into the left side only (so a mono signal keeps its level), each side's echo feeds the other side, and each bounce is **half** the delay time that Zone and Rate give (the module's manual says only that the repeats bounce between left and right; "half" is how Bryn's module was described). The dry signal is not summed: the stereo input still comes straight through under Mix. Swap leaves the inputs and the times alone and only crosses the feedback.
- **uRate and the LFO, in detail (first guesses, tuned by ear later):** the knob moves the delay by up to 6 ms either way in Zones 1 to 7 (a fixed time, the same in every Zone, as the module's uRate has a fixed range) and by +-12 semitones in Zone 0; the LFO swings up to +-12 ms (Zones 1 to 7) or +-12 semitones (Zone 0) at full Depth, on a squared Depth curve, 0.1 to 20 Hz, held back at fast speeds so the pitch swing never exceeds about -15 / +8 semitones; the synced LFO has ten divisions (the Clock Division list) with one cycle per division. The real module has no LFO (you patch one into its uRate input), so the knob, the LFO and these ranges are this plugin's own; the one thing taken from the module is the shape of the response (a small fixed range, linear in Zones 1 to 7, 1 V/oct in Zone 0, opposite on the right with Skew) and Bryn's description (very deep at the extremes, a nice chorus around the middle). The LFO has no start phase control: free-running, it just runs; synced, it follows Logic's song position while Logic plays.
- **Halo, in detail (first guesses, tuned by ear later):** the repeat that is heard (DC taken out) goes through four allpass diffusers per side and a feedback tank per side (a delay line, a damping low-pass, and a lossless rotation between left and right). Size grows with the knob: the tank line is 2.5 ms at the first touch (a plucked-string pitch) to 60 ms on the left and 68 ms on the right fully right, the allpass times grow from 15 % to full size, the loop gain goes from 0.30 to 0.88 (never above 0.9) and the rotation widens to about 65 degrees. The level of the halo is full from 70 % of the knob; at full level the repeat itself is turned down by half. Fully left is a dead zone (up to 0.4 %) where nothing at all is touched. Above 55 % the halo is also injected into the feedback (up to half of it, times the Repeats amount). Color's damping low-pass (8 kHz at unity, down to 700 Hz with a dark Color) and, on its thin side, a high-pass (up to 1.5 kHz) set the timbre. Things you may ask to retune: how tight the middle is, how long the hall rings, how wide, how loud the halo is next to the repeat, where the injection starts, whether the halo should also act when Halo is just above zero, the pitched sweep when the knob moves.
- **Mix is equal power:** at 50 % the live signal and the echoes are each 3 dB down. The percentage shown is the knob position.
- **Clear empties every Zone at once, with no pause in the audio** (it does not erase the memory, it marks everything written so far as silent). It acts once when the button goes on.
- **Processor cost** (measured on the Linux test computer, not on the Macs, in 0.5.0): about 0.4 % of one core for a stereo track at 48 kHz with Color at unity and about 0.7 % with Color turned either way (60 seconds of noise). Milestone 6 gives each side its own read point and milestone 7 adds an LFO and the uRate offset to every read, so expect a little more; it is measured again in milestone 12.
- **Tail:** Logic is told how long the echoes keep sounding with the current settings (about 60 dB of fall, plus the halo's ring when Halo is up; at infinite and beyond it says 120 seconds, the longest it will claim).
- **Tempo Sync, in detail (first guesses, tuned by ear later):** the clock is Logic's tempo at the Clock Division's note value (120 BPM when Logic gives none, never below 10 or above 999). The Rate knob is 13 equal bands (x2, x1 3/4, x1 2/3, x1 1/2, x1 1/3, x1 1/4, 1/1, /1 1/4, /1 1/3, /1 1/2, /1 2/3, /1 3/4, /2; the module's manual list). The time is the clock period divided by the step's factor, doubled for every Zone above the Zone that fits the clock best and halved for every Zone below it (the manual: "1/1 will be found at about 12:30 on the Zone most appropriate to the Tempo"; "multiply or divide by powers of 2 by changing Zones"); the best-fitting Zone is the one whose middle is nearest the clock period (a quarter note at 120 BPM fits Zone 4, a 16th fits Zone 2). **Because the Zone is relative to the clock, changing Clock Division between plain note values (1/4 to 1/8) does not change the time in the same Zone; it changes where the Zone's octave sits (use the Zone knob to move between them); triplets do change it.** A new step, Zone, Clock Division, the button itself, or the best-fitting Zone moving as Logic's tempo crosses a boundary is a **jump with a 20 ms crossfade, never a bend**; a tempo that merely drifts inside a Zone is followed by the normal Rate glide (160 ms). Each side of a Skew picks its own step. Ping Pong halves the clocked time. uRate and its LFO still bend the pitch (doppler is not removed by Tempo Sync). The TICK light still follows Logic's tempo at the Clock Division; the RATE light blinks at the clocked time.
- **The two read points:** if you change Zone again within a fiftieth of a second of the last change, the new change takes over the crossfade in progress (in theory a tiny discontinuity; you are unlikely to hear it).
- **The values are first guesses.** The curve and the corner frequencies of Color, the curve of Repeats (the "infinite" stretch is 0.80 to 0.86 on the knob), the Rate glide time, the crossfade time, the feedback's DC blocker and the soft limiter's knee can all be tuned by ear later without touching a control id.
- **Window choice is saved with the project, not as a control.** It does not appear in Logic's automation list.
- **Tempo comes from Logic.** The panel shows Logic's tempo while the plugin is processing audio (Logic does not run effects while it is stopped, so the box can lag behind a tempo change until you press Play).
- **Controls keep their ids forever.** If a later milestone ever needs one more control, it is added at the end, and older projects open with it at its starting position.
- **Starting positions can still change.** A new MimicPhon opens with the values in the window above; these may be tuned by ear in later milestones. Projects you save keep their own values.
- **The Clear button switches itself off** a moment after it goes on, and a project saved while Clear was on opens with it off.
- **Mono and stereo tracks are both supported.** Other channel layouts are refused by the plugin. **Skew and Ping Pong / Swap need a stereo track** (they do nothing on a mono one, and the window then shows one delay time and the plain PING PONG / SWAP label); left and right are otherwise independent.
- Plugin buttons act while Logic is playing: Logic does not run effects while it is stopped.

## What changed in 0.9.0

- **Halo** (milestone 9, `core/Halo.h`, `Engine`). Stereo diffusion of the repeat that is heard: four allpass diffusers and a cross-coupled feedback tank per side. **Fully left = nothing, bit for bit; middle = a tight, string-like reverb; fully right = a big, wide hall that always dies away** (the tank's loop gain stays below 0.9 and the left / right rotation loses nothing). **It works with Repeats fully left and Mix fully right** (Filter-Verb). **Above about 55 % it is also injected into the feedback** (scaled by Repeats). **Color sets its timbre** (dark = a dark reverb, thin = a thin one). Turned down, it fades in about a third of a second; Clear empties it; the host tail includes it.

## What changed in 0.8.0

## Folders

| Folder | What it is |
|---|---|
| `engine/` | The audio engine. Plain C++, no plugin code. `engine/include/mph/core/` holds the generic parts (the tempo clock from Logic, level helpers, the Zone / Rate time map, the delay memory, the Repeats law, Color, the Skew rules); `Engine.h` is the engine (the repeater) and `Params.h` the list of control values it understands, with the Zone table. `engine/tests/` holds the self-test. |
| `plugin/` | The plugin wrapper: what Logic sees, the list of controls (`Source/Parameters.h`, frozen), and the windows (`StyledPanel` and `PanelLookAndFeel` for the styled panel, `PluginEditor` for the plain one and the switch; fonts in `Fonts/`). `plugin/preview/` is an optional tool that draws the windows to pictures and checks the controls (not part of the plugin). |
| `tools/install/` | The generic installer used by all the "Unofficial" plugins (the same file in every plugin). |
| `.github/workflows/` | The automatic build. |

The wrapper hands the engine the control values (one plain `Params` list), Logic's tempo and the audio; the engine hands audio
back. Nothing in `engine/` knows about sliders or Logic.

## Getting the plugin

1. Open the **Actions** tab of this repo and click the latest **Build MimicPhon** run.
2. At the bottom, under **Artifacts**, download **MimicPhon-macOS** and unzip it (twice: the download is a zip containing `MimicPhon-macOS.zip`).
3. Inside the `MimicPhon-macOS` folder are `MimicPhon.component`, `MimicPhon.vst3`, `INSTALL.txt`, `auval.log` and
   `Install-Unofficial-Plugin.command`.
4. In Terminal, once, clear the download block: `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/MimicPhon-macOS/*`
   (adjust the path if you unzipped elsewhere).
5. **Easy install (Logic, Audio Unit):** double-click `Install-Unofficial-Plugin.command`. It asks for your Mac
   password once, installs the AU for all users and restarts the AU registrar.
   Or by hand: copy `MimicPhon.component` to `~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/` and `MimicPhon.vst3` to `~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/`.
6. Restart Logic. If it was already scanned, Logic Pro > Settings > Plug-In Manager > Reset & Rescan Selection.

The plugin appears under **Audio Units > particledots > MimicPhon**.
